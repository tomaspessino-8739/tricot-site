Direct answer

From an operational problem to an adopted, measurable change.

TRICOT's method turns an operational problem into an adopted, measurable change. We first observe the real work and structure its processes, data, owners and exceptions. We then configure or build the necessary layer, test it with users, implement it, train the team and measure its usefulness. Each stage has a gate: when a critical condition is missing, it is resolved before work moves forward.

Forward Deployed model

Strategy and execution belong to the same job.

A recommendation does not change daily work. A system does not either when it cannot be used, maintained or corrected inside the operation.

Forward Deployed describes delivery responsibility, not jargon or unlimited availability.

01The solution is designed away from the people doing the work.

02Test data does not represent real exceptions.

03Training arrives after everything is built.

04Nobody owns maintenance, costs or evolution.

05Success is declared at delivery rather than use.

The project does not end when it works for the builder. It ends when it works for the person who must use it.

A client may engage one stage or the full journey, but critical conditions are not skipped to create artificial speed.

Shared responsibilities

TRICOT can build the system. The client still owns the decisions.

TRICOT facilitates, proposes, implements, documents and raises risks. The client provides legitimate access, validates sources and rules, participates in testing and manages adoption with support.

01

Execution is shared. Responsibility does not remain vague.

FAQ

Questions about the TRICOT method.

The method adapts depth and deliverables without changing its essential gates.

What does Forward Deployed mean?

Working close to the operation and participating in execution until the solution can be used and measured.

Must every stage be contracted?

No. One stage may be engaged, but gates protecting the outcome are not skipped.

What does the client provide?

A decision owner, operational owner, information, legitimate access, validation and participation in testing and adoption.

When do you stop before building?

When a stable process, trusted source, permissions, ownership or success criteria are missing.

How is a solution tested?

Using agreed criteria, representative data, permissions, errors and tasks performed by the intended user.

Who maintains the solution?

The proposal and handoff define ownership, dependencies, costs, support and exit.
