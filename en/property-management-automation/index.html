Direct answer

Automation does not always mean replacing the PMS.

Property management automation connects properties, bookings, people, tasks, payments and reporting around one shared operation. TRICOT first determines what the PMS covers, which information lives elsewhere and which exceptions require a person. We then decide whether to configure, integrate or build a separate operational layer while keeping a clear source of truth.

Symptoms

The PMS records one part. The real work continues elsewhere.

A platform may manage bookings correctly while leaving owner reporting, collaborators, incidents, documents or financial decisions outside the workflow.

The problem becomes visible when nobody can reconstruct the operation without asking several people.

01Availability, rates or status differ between tools.

02Booking changes do not update tasks, payments or messages.

03Incidents are resolved in chat without an operational record.

04Owner reports are rebuilt manually.

05Exceptions depend on the person holding the context.

You do not need another database. You need to decide which one leads and how the others respond.

FAQ

Questions about property automation.

The specific answer depends on the PMS, channels, sources and roles involved.

Does TRICOT replace our PMS?

Not by default. We first determine what it solves well and which work remains outside it.

Which processes can a property manager automate?

Requests, bookings, tasks, incidents, documents, messages and reporting are candidates when rules and exceptions are defined.

Can you connect our current tools?

Only after verifying APIs, exports, permissions, limits and maintenance ownership.

When does custom software make sense?

When a central, repeated and stable gap cannot be solved reliably by the current stack.

Do you manage the properties directly?

No. TRICOT provides consulting, systems, automation and implementation, not physical property operations.

How do we begin?

With a reviewed form and, when there is a fit, a free initial consultation.
