Direct answer

The tool comes after the work is understood.

Automation consulting examines how a business process works before selecting tools or AI. TRICOT identifies inputs, sources, owners, workflow stages, exceptions and outcomes, compares the available options and can participate in implementation. The right recommendation may be to simplify, configure an existing tool, connect systems, build custom software or wait.

The problem

The tool often arrives before the right question.

Automation can execute exactly what was requested and still make the business worse when the source, rule or intended outcome was poorly defined.

The first decision is not which tool to buy. It is which change deserves to be made.

01The same information is entered into several tools.

02Automation fails when an exception appears.

03Nobody knows which source leads or who corrects an error.

04Technical activity is measured instead of operational change.

05AI is proposed before the decision it should improve is defined.

Not every repetitive task deserves automation. And not every manual process is well designed.

Five possible outcomes. Not one predetermined answer.

Impact, frequency, stability, data quality, error risk, reversibility and adoption determine the next step.

FAQ

Questions about automation consulting.

An assessment is not mandatory for every case, and building is not a predetermined outcome.

Is the initial consultation free?

Yes. It is used to understand the need and decide the next step; it is not a full audit.

How do you choose what to automate first?

We combine impact, frequency, stability, data, risk, reversibility and adoption capacity.

Do you work with our current tools?

Yes, when their capabilities, permissions and limits can solve the problem reliably.

Does the assessment include development?

Not by default. Implementation is defined as a separate scope or later stage.

What if automation is not appropriate?

The reason is recorded and the recommendation may be to simplify, measure, wait or keep the current process.

How is the result measured?

Using an agreed definition and source before implementation, separating adoption, observed outcomes and estimates.
